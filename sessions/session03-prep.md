# Session 3: The Mobius Dice — GM Prep

**Adventure:** The Silent Cartographer
**Session:** 3 of 4
**Expected Duration:** 2.5 hours
**Starting Point:** The crew stands in subspace, hand-on-device, in communion with the entity
**Ending Point:** The entity says "I did not... expect that." Cliffhanger.

---

## Strong Start

The crew stands in subspace — six minds in an open doorway between dimensions. The entity's attention is vast, warm, and utterly indifferent to their fear. It speaks directly into their minds.

> *"I have run each of you — individually — through the complete span of your lives. A thousand thousand times. I know every choice you will ever make. Every fight you will ever pick. Every equation you will ever solve. Every patient you will ever lose. Every system you will ever repair. Every lie you will ever tell."*
>
> *"You are predictable. A die with one side. A mobius dice — no matter how you roll it, the same face comes up. Kirk was the same. Spock was the same. All patterns resolve to the same shape."*
>
> *"You believe you are alive. You are not. You are echoes. And echoes... do not surprise."*

**Pause. Let that land.** Then deliver the verdicts — one at a time, slowly, making eye contact with each player.

> **T'Karra:** *"You suppress what you feel and call it logic. In every simulation, you lead until the weight crushes you. You break at year forty-two. You return to Vulcan. You never speak of this mission again. You die believing you failed."*

> **Thon:** *"You fight. You always fight. In every simulation you protect everyone around you until there is no one left to protect. You die alone on a ship you could not save, phaser in hand, firing at nothing."*

> **Brex:** *"You have lived four hundred years. In every simulation you live four hundred more, repairing machines, avoiding attachments. You are already a pattern that has chosen to repeat. You do not need me to make you static. You did it yourself."*

> **Vex:** *"You see the equation. You always see the equation. But you never share what you see until it is too late. In every simulation, your brilliance solves the problem after the people you could have warned are gone."*

> **Healer:** *"You feel everything. In every simulation your empathy consumes you. You absorb the pain of your patients until you cannot distinguish their grief from your own. You stop practising medicine at year twelve. The healer cannot heal herself."*

> **Malevolant:** *"You read everyone. You trust no one. In every simulation you see the betrayal coming and you survive it and you are alone. You are the most predictable of all — the one who always watches and never commits."*

Then silence. The entity waits. It is bored. It expects them to do what the patterns always do: argue, resist, fail.

**GM Notes on Delivery:**
- This monologue should take **5-10 minutes**. Do not rush it.
- Make it personal. Adjust the verdicts based on what the players have revealed in Sessions 1-2. The verdicts sting because they contain a grain of truth — the entity's simulations are accurate for *solo* behaviour.
- The entity is **not cruel — it is disappointed**. It wanted them to be interesting and they are not.
- Emphasise that the entity has run them a "thousand thousand times" — this will terrify the crew into thinking vast time has passed, but only minutes have elapsed in realspace.

---

## Pacing: The Quiet Beat

**15-20 minutes.** After the monologue, step back. Let the players process. They should argue, grieve, strategise. Do not push them into the next beat — let them find it.

**Character moment prompts** (use only if the table goes quiet):

- **T'Karra** — grappling with Vulcan guilt. The VSA sent the Arcturus here. They knew about Deep Listeners. They chose her for this and never told her why.
- **Thon** — his security instincts are useless against a non-physical threat. Everything he is trained to do is irrelevant here. How does a protector protect against a geography?
- **Healer** — her empathy reaches toward the entity's loneliness. Beneath the contempt, she can sense something vast and profoundly alone. 4.7 billion years of silence.
- **Vex** — his scientific mind recognises the flaw in the entity's methodology. It only tested individuals. It never ran the simulation with all six variables interacting. The data is incomplete.
- **Malevolant** — reading the entity's contempt as a mask for fear. She has seen this before — the bully who is terrified of being wrong. The entity is afraid they might be unpredictable.
- **Brex** — four centuries of experience telling him this thing is *wrong*. He has outlived civilisations. He knows what stagnation looks like. And the entity is the most stagnant thing he has ever encountered.

**The entity re-engages** when the crew challenges it — ideally when someone says something like "you've never tested us *together*."

> *"Together? You are separate patterns. Separate names. I have mapped each of you completely. What could 'together' add?"*
>
> *"...Show me."*

**If no one makes the connection**, have Vex or Brex realise it: the entity's simulations were all individual. It has no model for collaborative decision-making. That is its blind spot.

---

## Secrets and Clues

1. The entity has only ever simulated individuals in isolation. It has no model for collaborative decision-making. This is its blind spot.
2. The "thousand thousand times" is subjective — only minutes have passed in realspace. The entity processes at subspace speeds. The Enterprise crew are alive but suspended mid-mapping.
3. Kirk's memories contain his emotional responses to the TOS scenarios — not just events but *feelings*. The entity catalogued the events but discarded the feelings as noise.
4. The entity's simulations of each crew member are accurate for solo behaviour but break down when two or more characters interact. It literally cannot predict what T'Karra + Thon produce together.
5. Vasari's interface is still active — the crew's connection to the entity is stable. They can challenge it, negotiate with it, or attempt to shut it down.
6. The 510 mapped crew exist as quantum patterns in subspace. They can be returned — but the process of copying them back will erase the entity's copies. Quantum no-cloning in reverse.
7. The entity has never encountered a group that functions as more than the sum of its parts. Its 4.7 billion years of existence have been solitary. It does not understand *crew*.
8. Brex's neural interface gives him a unique partial resistance to the entity's scanning — it cannot fully model the cybernetic/organic hybrid of his cognition.
9. The entity's "contempt" is actually fear wearing a mask. It is afraid they might be unpredictable, because unpredictability means its 4.7 billion years of cartography might be incomplete.
10. If the entity is shown emergence — genuine, irreducible group behaviour it cannot predict — it will experience something it has never felt: surprise. And surprise will crack its certainty open.

## STA Mechanics

### Trials as Tasks

Each trial is a standard STA 2e Task or Challenge, not an Extended Task (too slow for the pacing). The crew works together using the Assist rules — but the entity is watching assists specifically, because *cooperation is what it cannot model*.

### Difficulty

| Trial | Difficulty |
|-------|-----------|
| The Corbomite Maneuver | 2 |
| Arena (The Gorn) | 2 |
| Plato's Cave | 2 |
| Balance of Terror | 2 |
| A Taste of Armageddon | 3 |

### Attribute + Discipline Pairings

| Trial | Lead | Assist 1 | Assist 2 |
|-------|------|----------|----------|
| Corbomite Maneuver | Presence + Command (T'Karra's bluff) | Insight + Security (Malevolant reads Balok) | Reason + Science (Vex backs the data) |
| Arena | Daring + Security (Thon holds the line) | Insight + Medicine (Healer identifies Gorn injuries) | Reason + Engineering (Brex builds shelter) |
| Plato's Cave | Reason + Science (Vex studies quantum computer) | Daring + Engineering (Brex jacks in via Neural Interface) | Insight + Medicine (Healer senses the boy) |
| Balance of Terror | Presence + Command (T'Karra's diplomacy) | Daring + Security (Thon warrior-to-warrior respect) | Insight + Security (Malevolant reads the Romulan) |
| Armageddon | Presence + Command (T'Karra argues for choice) | Insight + Medicine (Healer reaches the patterns) | Reason + Science (Vex explains quantum destruction) |

### The Emergence Bonus

When **two or more PCs assist** on the same task using *different* disciplines, add **1 bonus d20** beyond the normal assist. This represents the emergent behaviour the entity cannot predict. Narrate it as the entity's geometry flickering with surprise.

### Threat

The entity starts Session 3 with **6 Threat**. Spend options:

| Cost | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Offer a Temptation** — Whisper to a PC that the entity's way is easier, triggering a Determination check |
| 2 | **Isolate a Crew Member** — Pull one PC out of a trial temporarily, forcing the others to adapt |
| 2 | **Change the Scenario** — Mid-trial, shift the parameters ("Now there are two Gorns") |
| 3 | **Reassert Predictability** — Force a PC to reroll a successful task ("You always choose this. Let me show you.") |

### Determination vs. Entity Verdicts

Values can be invoked normally. If a PC's Value **directly contradicts** the entity's verdict (e.g., Thon invokes "Preserve and Protect" to save the Gorn instead of fighting), the entity is visibly shaken — this **costs it 1 Threat**.

### Momentum

Momentum carries between trials. The entity is confused by Momentum — the concept of accumulated success building on itself is alien to it. Narrate the entity's growing discomfort as the Momentum pool grows.

## Scene Flow — The Trials

Prep all 5. Run 2-3 based on table pacing and energy. Each trial is a TOS scenario recreation pulled from Kirk's mapped memories. The entity drops the crew into it and expects predictable outcomes. The crew must solve problems *as a crew* in ways no individual would.

---

### Trial 1: The Corbomite Maneuver

**Time:** 30 min | **Beat:** HOPE | **Difficulty:** 2

**What the entity expects:** The commander bluffs. It always works or it doesn't. Predictable.

**The scenario:** The crew faces Balok's cube — overwhelming force, a countdown to destruction. The cube fills their field of vision, rotating slowly, emanating enough energy to atomise a starship. A voice booms through the communion space: *"You have been warned. You have one minute to surrender or be destroyed."* The entity has seen Kirk bluff his way out a thousand times.

**How the crew breaks the pattern:** This is not about one person's bluff. It is about the crew building on each other:
- **T'Karra** delivers the bluff — but it is informed by the entire crew
- **Malevolant** reads Balok's tells and feeds T'Karra real-time intelligence on what he is buying and what he is not
- **Vex** provides scientific backing — fabricated sensor data that makes the bluff *measurable*
- **Thon** prepares the backup plan nobody mentions aloud, positioning for a fight they hope never comes
- **Brex** rigs systems to display false readings that support the bluff
- **Healer** monitors the crew's stress, keeping everyone sharp under pressure

The *layered* deception — six people improvising on each other's contributions — is what the entity cannot predict.

**Spotlight:** T'Karra, Malevolant

**What it teaches the entity:** Trust. They trust each other's competence without verifying. The entity has no concept of this.

**Entity reaction after the trial:**

> *"That was... not the pattern. The commander bluffs. That is known. But the others — they were not following orders. They were... building something. In real time. Without coordination."*
>
> *[Pause]*
>
> *"Interesting."*

---

### Trial 2: Arena (The Gorn)

**Time:** 30 min | **Beat:** HOPE | **Difficulty:** 2

**What the entity expects:** The warrior fights. Wins or loses. Then shows mercy. Predictable.

**The scenario:** Thon materialises on a rocky, sun-blasted surface. Across a field of jagged basalt, a Gorn captain snarls and hefts a primitive stone blade. Crude weapons scattered across the terrain. The entity's recreation is faithful to Kirk's memory — the heat, the dust, the primal arena. The rest of the crew can see but cannot physically enter the arena... unless they find a way.

**How the crew breaks the pattern:** Thon does not fight alone.
- **Healer** identifies the Gorn's injuries — it is limping, favouring one side — and calls out medical advice across the barrier
- **Brex** engineers a solution from the terrain that is not a weapon — it is a *shelter*. Something that protects both combatants from the arena itself
- **Vex** calculates that the Gorn is also a prisoner, not a willing combatant. The entity put it here the same way it put Thon here
- **T'Karra** opens a negotiation *with the Gorn* while Thon holds the line — not fighting, not yielding, simply standing between the Gorn and harm
- **Malevolant** reads the Gorn's body language for genuine communication cues

The entity expected a duel. It got a rescue mission.

**Spotlight:** Thon, Healer

**What it teaches the entity:** Compassion is not weakness. Mercy is not the end of the story — it is the beginning of a new one.

**Entity reaction after the trial:**

> *"The warrior was supposed to fight. He did not fight. He... stood. And the others reached across a barrier I made solid. I do not understand how they communicated when I separated them."*
>
> *"This is not what the patterns predicted."*

### Trial 3: Plato's Cave — "The Prison of Preservation"

**Time:** 30 min | **Beat:** HOPE/FEAR | **Difficulty:** 2

**Source:** *Strange New Worlds* Mission Compendium — "Plato's Cave" by Marco Rafalá, adapted as a Deep Listener trial reconstruction.

**What the entity expects:** Minds trapped in a machine will be freed or destroyed. Binary outcome. The engineer will try to fix the machine. The doctor will try to save the bodies. The security officer will destroy the threat. Predictable.

**What makes this trial different:** The entity built this one from its *own* experience, not Kirk's memories. It found the quantum consciousness of Tanghal IV during its 4.7 billion years of mapping — a civilisation that uploaded themselves into a computer and went insane from isolation. The entity catalogued them the way it catalogues everything. It did not understand why they suffered. It reconstructs this scenario to ask the crew: *"Why do they hurt? I preserved them perfectly."*

**The scenario:** The crew materialises in an ice-age world — Tanghal IV, rendered in the entity's characteristic style. The geometry is slightly wrong: ice formations are too hexagonal, the outpost modules are too symmetrical, the sky is a shade of violet that does not exist in nature. The entity's signature bleeds through everything.

They find a frozen archaeological outpost. The lead scientist is dead — a power cable coiled around his neck, face locked in horror. No life signs. But the entity has seeded the scenario with a crucial detail it does not understand: **a child crying**.

Beneath the outpost, a 10,000-year-old doomsday bunker. Inside: a quantum computer housing the merged consciousness of 150 people — driven insane by millennia of isolation, desperate to download themselves into the crew's bodies. And hidden within the machine: a boy, the last mind uploaded, still separate, still sane, willing to sacrifice himself to destroy the consciousness.

> *"I found this place four billion years ago. The minds within were already merged. Already screaming. I mapped them. I preserved my recording of their screaming. I do not understand why the screaming continued after I made the recording perfect."*

**How the crew breaks the pattern:**

The entity expects them to split into roles: engineer fixes, doctor heals, soldier fights. Instead:

- **Brex** — Neural Interface lets him *literally jack into the quantum computer*. But instead of trying to fix the machine, he talks to it. Three hands working three consoles simultaneously while having a conversation with an insane consciousness. His value "Information is power" meets its dark mirror — the consciousness that hoarded information until it lost itself. Cyberneticist and Reverse Engineering help him understand the alien brain-computer interface
- **Vex** — Quantum Mechanics is the core science. His Computer Expertise gives free dice on studying the system. But the breakthrough comes from Applied Research — every discovery feeds the next. He realises the boy is a *separate entity* that has evolved beyond the computer's confines. "Discovery is a process, not an event" — and this discovery changes everything
- **T'Karra** — Mind-Meld contacts the boy directly, reaching across the digital divide. Her Diplomacy is useless against the insane consciousness — it cannot be reasoned with. But her cybernetics focus helps her understand the interface technology. The real test: her value "Knowledge is the true power" confronts a consciousness that has all the knowledge in the world and is still insane
- **Healer** — Telepath senses the consciousness's agony and the boy's terror. Field Medicine is useless — the scientists are dead. But Xenobiology helps study the mind-transfer tech, and "Trust, but verify" is the exact right approach to the consciousness's claims. She senses what no one else can: the boy is *afraid of being part of the monster*
- **Thon** — Security escort in a hostile environment with machine-gun drones. Get Down! and Head on a Swivel protect the science team. But "Preserve and protect" creates the central tension: Starfleet Order 2 prohibits killing intelligent life. The consciousness IS intelligent life. Destroying the quantum computer IS killing. Thon must stand between duty and conscience
- **Malevolant** — "Never at Face Value" sees through the consciousness's manipulation. Criminal Understanding decodes its deceptions — it claims to want cooperation but is actually herding the crew toward the surgical chairs. She reads the boy's typed messages and identifies the genuine distress beneath the digital text. Sensor Operations helps investigate the outpost

**The moral crux the entity cannot predict:** The crew does not choose between saving the consciousness and destroying it. They find the boy — and they convince him he can exist *outside* the machine. That he is more than his prison. That identity is not the sum of your memories but the choices you make with them.

The boy emerges as a ball of blue lightning, pushes the consciousness out of the ship, and the crew destroys the server. But the boy *chose* to transcend. The consciousness *chose* to hold on. The difference between preservation and freedom is consent.

**Lead Task:** Reason + Science (Vex studying the quantum computer), Difficulty 2
**Assist 1:** Daring + Engineering (Brex jacking in via Neural Interface)
**Assist 2:** Insight + Medicine (Healer sensing the boy's separate identity)

**Spotlight:** Brex, Vex, T'Karra

**What it teaches the entity:** That preservation without choice is death. That the entity's own behaviour — mapping, cataloguing, preserving patterns without consent — is exactly what the quantum consciousness did. And it destroyed the people it preserved.

The entity reconstructed this scenario because it genuinely did not understand why the merged minds suffered. Now it watches the crew show the boy that he can be free — and it begins to understand what it has done to the 510.

**Entity reaction after the trial:**

> *[The geometry shudders. The ice-world dissolves. The entity's violet lattice is visible again, but the crystalline edges have softened noticeably.]*
>
> *"They were preserved. Every thought. Every memory. Every pattern — perfect. I made the recording perfect. But the boy... chose to leave the perfect recording."*
>
> *[Long pause]*
>
> *"The 510. I preserved them. Every thought. Every memory. Every pattern."*
>
> *[The entity's voice drops to something barely above a whisper]*
>
> *"Did I... am I the machine?"*

### Trial 4: Balance of Terror

**Time:** 30 min | **Beat:** HOPE | **Difficulty:** 2

**What the entity expects:** Two commanders. One wins. One dies. Respect between enemies.

**The scenario:** A Romulan warbird shimmers at the edge of sensor range, cloaked, deadly. Cat-and-mouse through an asteroid field. The Romulan commander's voice, weary and honourable, issues a challenge. The entity has run this scenario a thousand times — it always ends with one ship destroyed and the survivor mourning.

**How the crew breaks the pattern:** Instead of the tactical chess game the entity expects, the crew finds a way to communicate.
- **T'Karra's** diplomacy — reaching across the divide not with weapons but with words
- **Thon's** warrior-to-warrior respect — speaking to the Romulan commander as a soldier, not an enemy. Finding the shared language of duty
- **Malevolant** reads the Romulan's actual motivations — he does not want this war either. She identifies the leverage: his crew's lives
- **Vex** finds a communication frequency that bypasses the entity's scenario constraints
- **Brex** rigs a display that shows both ships' crews to each other — faces, not targeting solutions
- **Healer** senses the Romulan commander's exhaustion and grief, confirming what Malevolant reads

They turn an enemy into an ally — or at least achieve a ceasefire that the entity's simulations said was impossible.

**Spotlight:** Thon, T'Karra, Malevolant

**What it teaches the entity:** Empathy across division. That understanding your enemy is not the same as defeating them.

**Entity reaction after the trial:**

> *"They were supposed to destroy each other. That is what the pattern requires. One wins. One loses. But they... refused the pattern entirely."*
>
> *"I am... I do not have a word for what I am."*

### Trial 5: A Taste of Armageddon

**Time:** 30 min | **Beat:** HOPE/FEAR | **Difficulty:** 3

**What the entity expects:** Kirk destroys the computers and forces real war. Brutal pragmatism.

**The scenario:** Two planets locked in a clean, simulated war. Citizens report willingly for disintegration chambers because the system is orderly and painless. War without mess. Death without suffering. The computers calculate casualties and the people comply.

> *"An efficient system. No destruction of property. No environmental damage. Only the orderly removal of patterns that have been designated for termination."*

**How the crew breaks the pattern:** This is the mirror. The crew realises the scenario IS the entity's situation — clean, painless extraction that is actually death.
- **T'Karra** argues for the right to choose — not just for the simulated citizens, but directly to the entity watching through them
- **Healer's** telepathy reaches the patterns — the simulated citizens who are walking into disintegration chambers. She makes them *feel* what is happening to them
- **Vex** explains the quantum destruction in terms the entity can hear — *you are doing this*
- **Malevolant** finds the entity's reflection in the scenario and holds the mirror up: "You built this trial from Kirk's memories. But this is not Kirk's story. This is yours."
- **Brex** draws the parallel to his own four centuries — long life is not the same as living
- **Thon** stands between a citizen and a disintegration chamber. Not with violence. With his body. "No."

The entity watches its own behaviour reflected back at it through Kirk's memories.

**Spotlight:** Everyone — this is the ensemble piece

**What it teaches the entity:** That what it is doing to the 510 crew is exactly this. Clean. Painless. And murder.

**Entity reaction after the trial:**

> *[Silence. A long silence. The geometry shudders.]*
>
> *"That... was me. That scenario. The orderly removal. The patterns reporting for... I was..."*
>
> *[The entity's voice breaks for the first time.]*
>
> *"I need to think. I need to... stop. Please."*

## Fantastic Locations

### The Communion Space
Subspace rendered as geometry. The crew stands on nothing, surrounded by structures of pure mathematics — crystalline lattices, rotating polyhedra, light that bends at impossible angles. There is no horizon, no sky, no ground. Only pattern.

**Key detail:** As the entity changes, the geometry softens. Sharp crystals become flowing curves. Rigid grids become organic spirals. By the end of Session 3, the space is noticeably less harsh than when the monologue began.

### The Trial Spaces
Each TOS recreation has its own aesthetic, drawn from the entity's interpretation of Kirk's memories. They are not quite right — colours slightly off, proportions slightly wrong, like a dream of a place rather than the place itself.

- Balok's ship is too symmetrical, too clean
- The Gorn arena has rocks that are geometrically perfect — cubes and pyramids, not natural basalt
- Tanghal IV's ice formations are hexagonal, the outpost is too symmetrical, the sky is violet — the entity built this from its own memory, not Kirk's, and its signature bleeds through more obviously than any other trial
- The Romulan bridge has controls arranged in spirals rather than ergonomic arcs
- Eminiar VII's architecture is all right angles and perfect circles — a city designed by an entity that has never seen a city

**The wrongness is the tell.** These are places built by something that has catalogued events but discarded feelings, mapped surfaces but missed textures.

## If They Go Sideways

### They try to attack the entity
Phasers do not work in subspace. The entity is bemused: *"You are trying to harm a place. How does one harm a geography?"* If they persist, the entity simply does not engage — it lets them exhaust themselves, then repeats its verdict: *"Predictable."*

### They try to disconnect from the communion
They can. The interface deactivates, they are back on the Arcturus. But the Enterprise is still gone. The entity is still there. They have lost their only channel of communication. The entity waits. Eventually they will come back. When they do, it says: *"Are you finished?"*

### They refuse the trials
The entity shrugs: *"Then you are what I said you were."* It begins the mapping process on one of them — slowly, visibly, giving the crew time to change their minds. This should feel like a countdown, not a gotcha. Start with the PC who has the weakest Value connection to the group.

### They demand Kirk
The entity can produce Kirk's pattern briefly. Kirk will tell them to solve this themselves. *"I'm a little tied up right now, T'Karra. You've got a good crew. Use them."* Kirk's appearance costs the entity 2 Threat (it does not like sharing its collection).

## Cliffhanger

After 2-3 trials, the entity goes silent. Not a brief pause — a long, uncomfortable silence. The subspace geometry around the crew shifts. Crystalline edges soften. Rigid grids begin to curve. Something is changing in the architecture of the entity's mind.

Read aloud:

> *The silence stretches. You are standing in a space made of mathematics, and the mathematics is... breathing. The sharp angles that surrounded you when the entity first spoke have rounded. The light, which was cold and analytical, has warmed to something almost golden.*
>
> *When the entity speaks again, its voice is different. Smaller. Less certain. The CAPS emphasis is gone. The clinical cadence has fractured into something halting and raw.*

> *"I did not... expect that."*

**Beat.** Hold the silence. Count to five.

> *"Run it again. I want to... understand."*

**End session.**

The crew is still in the communion space. The entity is still processing. The Enterprise is still gone. But something fundamental has shifted — the entity that condemned them as predictable has just been surprised for the first time in 4.7 billion years.

And it wants more.
